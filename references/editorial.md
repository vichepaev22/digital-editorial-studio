# Evidence and editorial method

- Inventory sources: author, title, edition, publication date, URL, page numbering convention, rights, retrieval date. Distinguish PDF physical page and printed page.
- Claim ledger columns: claim_id, claim, source_id, page/section, unit, population, period, denominator, status (verified / attributed / disputed / editorial inference / unverified), caveat, reviewer. Every material quantitative claim must resolve to a source.
- For reported estimates, preserve attribution and assumptions. Do not convert expert opinion to standards, or hypothetical scenarios to measured outcomes. Do not silently update historical findings with newer data.
- Review visual encodings: baseline, scale, denominators, missing values, uncertainty, comparability. Provide data table or text alternative. Avoid fake precision and rankings across incompatible tests.
- Editorial sequence: cover and thesis, short reader route, context, evidence, mechanisms, applications, limitations, sources. Prefer plain language; introduce terms where used.
- Quotation and image rights: verify license or permission, credit creator, keep a rights ledger; distinguish generated illustrations from source figures.
- Reader test: recruit representative readers; ask them to find the main conclusion, interpret a number with its caveat, locate its source and complete a useful action. Report sample size, failures and observations, not universal claims.
